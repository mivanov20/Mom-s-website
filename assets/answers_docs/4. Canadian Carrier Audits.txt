4. CANADIAN CARRIER AUDITS
What is a New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) in Alberta?
A New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) is required for new Alberta commercial carriers that go through the Pre-Entry Program and receive a Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC).
The review looks at the records you've created since you started operating and whether your company is following Alberta's commercial carrier requirements.
When do I need to complete my NCCR?
The NCCR must be completed within 9 to 12 months after your Safety Fitness Certificate is issued.
What records are checked during an NCCR?
The review may include records such as:
safety and maintenance programs
driver files
Hours of Service records and supporting documents
trip inspection reports
vehicle files
maintenance and repair records
insurance
carrier profile
These records show how the company has been operating since you received your SFC.
Is an NCCR the same as a facility audit?
No.
An NCCR is required for new Alberta carriers going through the Pre-Entry Program and looks at the company's compliance during its first year of operation.
A facility audit is a more detailed examination of the carrier's safety and compliance records.
Can an NCCR be done remotely?
Yes.
An NCCR can be completed in person or remotely. If it is completed remotely, the required records can be provided electronically.
What happens if I don't complete my NCCR?
If you don't complete the NCCR by the assigned deadline, your Safety Fitness Certificate can be suspended.
While the SFC is suspended, the company's NSC-regulated vehicles cannot legally operate until the required review has been completed and the submitted documents have been reviewed.
How soon after receiving my Alberta Safety Fitness Certificate will I have my New Carrier Compliance Review?
If you received a new Alberta Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC) through the Pre-Entry Program, you are required to complete a New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) within 9 to 12 months after your SFC is issued. The review must be completed before the first anniversary of your Safety Fitness Certificate.
When should I start preparing for the NCCR?
You should be maintaining the required records from the beginning of your operations. By the time your NCCR is due, you will need records showing how your safety and maintenance programs have been followed in practice.
These may include driver files, Hours of Service records and supporting documents, trip inspection reports, vehicle and maintenance records, and other records required for your operation.
Do I need to arrange the NCCR myself?
The NCCR is a requirement attached to the new carrier process. The carrier is responsible for making sure the review is completed within the required timeframe.
Your assignment information will provide the details and deadline that apply to your review.
What happens if I don't complete my NCCR on time?
If the NCCR is not completed by the assigned deadline, your Safety Fitness Certificate will be suspended. While the SFC is suspended, you cannot legally operate your NSC commercial vehicles.
The NCCR needs to be completed and the required documentation reviewed before the suspension can be lifted.

I received a New Carrier Compliance Review notice. What do I do now?
If you received a New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) notice in Alberta, start by identifying the deadline and the records being requested. The review examines your records and reports on your company's demonstrated compliance with National Safety Code safety standards and Alberta transportation requirements.
What records should I prepare?
Gather the records requested for the review and make sure they cover the period being examined. This may include your safety and maintenance programs, driver files, Hours of Service records and supporting documents, trip inspection reports, vehicle files, maintenance and repair records, insurance information and carrier profile.
What if some of my records are missing?
Identify missing or incomplete records before anything is submitted. Determine whether the documents can be located and whether additional information is needed. Do not create or alter records to make it appear that something was done when it was not.
Should I review my files before submitting them?
Yes. Review the complete file before submission so you know what the records show and whether there are gaps or inconsistencies. If a problem is found, determine what can properly be corrected and what needs to be explained.
Is the NCCR done in person or remotely?
An NCCR may be completed remotely or in person. The notice and the arrangements for your review will determine how your records need to be provided and how the review will be conducted.
What happens after the NCCR?
Once the review is completed, the results are reported to Alberta Transportation and Economic Corridors. If additional information is required during the review, you may be asked to provide it.
What does a positive NCCR result mean?
A positive NCCR result means the review demonstrated an acceptable level of compliance with the requirements being examined. The NCCR requirement has been completed and the result is reported to Alberta Transportation and Economic Corridors.
What happens if my NCCR result is negative?
A negative NCCR result does not automatically mean that you have to stop operating. The result identifies compliance problems found during the review and is reported to Alberta Transportation and Economic Corridors.
Depending on the issues identified, the carrier may be subject to further compliance action or conditions that need to be completed. If a condition is assigned, it must be completed by the required deadline.
Does the NCCR change my Safety Fitness Rating?
New Alberta carriers generally begin with a Satisfactory Unaudited Safety Fitness Rating. A positive or negative NCCR result should not be described as automatically changing the carrier's Safety Fitness Rating.
A Satisfactory rating is tied to Alberta's requirements for an acceptable NSC audit and other applicable criteria. The NCCR and an NSC Facility Audit are not the same process.
What happens if I miss the NCCR deadline?
New carriers going through Alberta's Pre-Entry Program must complete the NCCR within 9 to 12 months after their Safety Fitness Certificate is issued.
If the requirement is not completed by the assigned deadline, the Safety Fitness Certificate is suspended on the first business day after the due date. The carrier's NSC vehicles cannot operate until the required review has been completed and the submitted documents have been reviewed.
How much time do I have to respond to an audit or review notice?
There is not one response period that applies to every audit, review or compliance notice. The letter you receive will tell you what is required and the deadline you need to meet.
Do not assume that you have a certain number of days because another carrier did. The deadline depends on the type of notice and the condition or action Alberta has assigned to your carrier.
Where do I find the deadline?
Check the audit, review or condition letter carefully. It will identify what you are required to complete and the due date assigned to the carrier.
The deadline may relate to completing an audit or review, submitting documents, paying an administrative penalty or meeting another condition placed on the carrier.
How much time do I have to complete a New Carrier Compliance Review?
New carriers going through Alberta's Pre-Entry Program must complete the New Carrier Compliance Review within 9 to 12 months of being issued their Safety Fitness Certificate. The NCCR is due before the first anniversary of the SFC.
If Alberta sends you an NCCR assignment or condition letter, follow the specific due date shown in that notice.
How much time do I have if Alberta orders a facility audit?
If Alberta assigns a facility audit as a condition on your Safety Fitness Certificate, the assignment letter gives you the due date for completing it.
The carrier needs to arrange the audit, prepare the required records and make sure the assigned condition is completed by that date.
What if Alberta asks me to submit additional documents?
Follow the deadline given in the request. A carrier may be assigned a condition with a specific submission due date, and that date needs to be met.
If you are unsure what needs to be submitted or when it is due, clarify the requirement before the deadline passes.
What happens if I miss the deadline?
If a carrier fails to complete an assigned condition by the due date, its Safety Fitness Certificate is suspended on the next business day after the due date. The carrier's regulated National Safety Code vehicles cannot operate while the SFC is suspended.
If the missed deadline is for payment of an administrative penalty, Alberta can instead restrict the carrier's services at Alberta registries on the next business day after the payment due date.
Can I ask for more time?
Do not assume that a deadline will automatically be extended. If there is a problem completing the assigned requirement, contact Alberta's Monitoring and Compliance office before the due date and follow the instructions provided for your particular notice.
Unless Alberta changes the condition or due date, treat the date shown in the notice as the deadline you are required to meet.
What should I do when I receive an audit or review notice?
Read the entire notice before responding. Identify what has been assigned, what records are required, who needs to complete the work and the exact due date.
Start preparing the records early. Waiting until the deadline is close can make it much harder to correct missing files, obtain documents from drivers or third parties, or arrange a certified auditor or reviewer when one is required.

What happens if the auditor finds deficiencies?
If an auditor finds deficiencies, they are documented in the audit report, along with the areas the carrier needs to correct. Finding deficiencies does not automatically mean your Safety Fitness Certificate will be suspended or that you will receive a penalty.
The audit results are used together with your Carrier Profile and compliance history to determine whether further action is required.
Will the auditor explain what was wrong?
Yes. A facility audit includes a meeting with the carrier after the audit is completed to review the results. The audit report shows the areas that were examined, the carrier's level of compliance and the deficiencies that were identified.
This gives the carrier an opportunity to understand what was found and what needs to be corrected.
Do I have to correct the deficiencies?
Yes. The carrier is expected to remedy the deficiencies identified in the audit report.
The purpose is not simply to correct the paperwork that was reviewed during the audit. If the deficiency shows that a safety or maintenance procedure is not working properly, the carrier needs to correct the underlying process as well.
What is an action item?
An action item identifies something the carrier needs to correct as a result of the audit.
For example, if required driver records are missing, the immediate action may be to obtain the records. If the records were missing because the company was not monitoring expiry dates or annual requirements, the carrier also needs to correct that process.
Do I need a corrective action plan?
The audit report can include action plan items identifying areas that need to be corrected. The carrier should be able to show how the deficiencies were addressed and how its procedures were changed where necessary.
The goal is to bring the carrier's actual safety and maintenance practices into compliance, not simply to prepare documents after the audit.
Does the auditor decide whether I get a penalty?
No. The auditor examines the carrier's records and reports the demonstrated level of compliance. Alberta Transportation and Economic Corridors determines whether disciplinary action is required.
Alberta reviews the facility audit report together with the carrier's profile when determining what should happen next.
What can Alberta do after reviewing the audit?
Depending on the carrier's audit results and compliance history, Alberta can take disciplinary action such as changing the carrier's Safety Fitness Rating, assigning follow-up conditions or issuing an administrative penalty.
The response depends on the carrier's overall compliance situation rather than simply on the fact that one deficiency was found.
What if Alberta gives me conditions to complete?
If Alberta assigns a condition, the carrier must complete it by the submission due date shown in the notice.
If the carrier fails to meet that deadline, its Safety Fitness Certificate can be suspended on the next business day after the due date. The carrier's regulated National Safety Code vehicles cannot operate while the SFC is suspended.
Should I keep records showing what I corrected?
Yes. Keep records showing what was corrected, when it was corrected and what changes were made to prevent the problem from happening again.
The important part after an audit is not simply that the carrier received the report. It is that the deficiencies identified in the report are actually corrected.
Can someone deal with the auditor on my behalf?
Yes. You can authorize someone to assist you with an audit or compliance matter and communicate or submit information on your behalf. Alberta allows a carrier to authorize a third-party representative to receive or submit carrier information.
Having someone represent you does not transfer the carrier's responsibilities to that person. The carrier remains responsible for its records, safety and maintenance systems and compliance with the requirements that apply to its operation.
Do I need to authorize someone to act on my behalf?
Yes. If you want Alberta Transportation and Economic Corridors to release carrier information to another person or allow that person to submit information on your behalf, the carrier may need to complete an Authorization to Release/Submit Information.
The authorization identifies the person who can receive or submit information for the carrier and the information they are authorized to handle.
Do I have to deal with the auditor myself?
Not necessarily. An authorized representative can assist with communication, organize the information being requested and coordinate documents on behalf of the carrier.
The carrier or its management may still need to participate when information, explanations, decisions or signatures are required directly from the company.
Can someone prepare my documents for the audit?
Yes. Someone familiar with carrier compliance can review and organize your driver files, Hours of Service records, vehicle inspection and maintenance records, safety and maintenance programs and other documents before the audit.
This can identify missing or incomplete records before they are reviewed. Missing records should be addressed honestly; documents should never be created or altered to make it appear that a requirement was completed when it was not.
Can someone submit the documents for me?
Yes. An authorized representative can submit carrier information on your behalf when the appropriate authorization is in place.
The records still belong to the carrier's compliance system. Having someone else organize or submit them does not change the carrier's responsibility for ensuring that the information is true, accurate and complete.
Can someone answer the auditor's questions for me?
A representative can communicate about the records and assist with questions relating to the audit process. However, the auditor may need information from the carrier's owner, management, drivers or other people involved in the operation.
A representative cannot change what actually happened or replace information that must come from the carrier and its records.
Can the person helping me also conduct the audit?
Audit preparation and conducting the official audit are different roles. An official facility audit required by Alberta must be conducted through Alberta's audit process by an Alberta Transportation investigator or a certified Third Party Auditor, as applicable.
Alberta's certification of an auditor applies to the auditing services they are authorized to conduct. Other services they may provide, such as consulting, training, internal monitoring or program development, are separate from that government certification.
Will I still need to participate in the audit?
Yes, when required. Even if a representative handles much of the preparation and communication, the carrier may still need to provide information, review the audit results and complete actions required as part of the audit.
At the end of a facility audit, the auditor reviews the results with the carrier. The carrier is responsible for correcting the regulatory deficiencies identified in the audit.
Who is responsible if something is missing?
The carrier remains responsible. If a required record is missing, incomplete or inaccurate, using a representative does not transfer that compliance responsibility to the representative.
A representative can identify the problem, help obtain legitimate records, organize the response and assist with corrective action, but the carrier remains responsible for meeting its regulatory obligations.
Can someone continue assisting me after the audit is finished?
Yes. A representative can continue assisting with follow-up documents, responses and corrective action after the audit.
The carrier still needs to understand what was found and make sure the identified deficiencies are corrected. Representation can assist with the process, but the carrier remains responsible for the operation and its compliance.

Can someone handle my audit if they didn't set up my compliance system?
Yes. The person assisting you with an audit does not have to be the person who originally set up your compliance system. Alberta recognizes that carriers may use consultants for assistance with their safety and compliance practices.
Before someone can properly assist with your audit, however, they need to understand how your carrier operates, what systems you currently use and what records you actually have. That normally begins with reviewing the existing files before anything is submitted for the audit.
Do they need to review my compliance system first?
Yes. Someone taking over an audit should review the carrier's existing safety and maintenance programs, driver files, Hours of Service records, vehicle inspection and maintenance records and other documents that apply to the operation.
These are the same general areas Alberta examines during a facility audit. Reviewing them first helps identify what is already in place and where the carrier may have deficiencies that need to be addressed.
What if someone else prepared my safety and maintenance programs?
That is not a problem by itself. Anyone can prepare a carrier's safety program as long as it meets the applicable requirements.
What matters is whether the programs apply to your actual operation and whether the company is following them. Alberta requires carriers not only to have written safety and maintenance programs, but to implement them in the business.
What if my records are incomplete?
Incomplete records should be identified before the audit wherever possible. Some missing documents may already exist and simply need to be obtained or organized. Other gaps may show that a required inspection, review, training activity or procedure was never completed.
Those situations need to be handled differently. Legitimate missing records can be recovered where possible, but records should not be created, altered or backdated to make it appear that something happened when it did not.
Can problems be fixed before the audit?
Yes. Carriers should review their own compliance and correct problems when they find them. Alberta specifically encourages carriers to conduct their own compliance reviews rather than waiting until poor safety performance results in government intervention.
An expired document may be renewed, a legitimate missing record may be obtained, and a procedure that is not working can be changed. Correcting the system now, however, does not change what happened during the period being audited.
Can my compliance system be changed before the audit?
Yes. If the review shows that part of your safety or maintenance system does not meet the requirements, does not fit your operation or is not being followed, it should be corrected.
The revised system should reflect how the company actually operates and how it will meet its requirements going forward. The purpose is not to rewrite the program so the audit file looks better. The carrier must actually follow the policies and procedures contained in its programs.
What if I use a third-party or electronic system for my records?
Carrier records can be maintained using paper, electronic or a combination of record-keeping systems. Using an ELD platform, maintenance provider or another electronic system does not remove the carrier's responsibility for its records.
The carrier needs to make sure its required records are organized, complete, up to date and available when they are needed.
Who is responsible for records created before someone takes over my audit?
The carrier remains responsible for its records and compliance. Alberta requires the carrier to maintain its required records and to designate someone responsible for implementing its safety program and ensuring compliance with safety laws.
A new consultant or representative cannot change the carrier's past compliance history. They can review what exists, identify deficiencies, organize legitimate records, assist with corrections and support the carrier through the audit process.
Does someone need to rebuild my entire compliance system before handling the audit?
Not necessarily. If the existing system meets the requirements, applies to the carrier's actual operation and is being followed, there may be no reason to replace it.
The first step is to review what you already have. From there, the areas that are missing, outdated, incomplete or not working properly can be addressed before the audit.
