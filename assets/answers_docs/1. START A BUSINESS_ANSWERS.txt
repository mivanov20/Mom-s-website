Featured 1: What do I need to start and run a trucking company in Alberta?
Starting a trucking company means setting up both the business and the trucking operation.
Do I need to incorporate?
Not necessarily. An owner-operator can run the business as a sole proprietor or through a corporation. As a sole proprietor, you and the business are legally the same. With a corporation, the company is a separate legal entity.
Whichever structure you choose, the business needs to be properly set up with the CRA accounts that apply to it and financial records from the beginning.
Will you stay in Alberta or haul outside the province?
This decision matters when you set up the trucking operation. If you operate only within Alberta, you may have Provincial Operating Status. If you operate outside Alberta, you may need Federal Operating Status.
What is a Safety Fitness Certificate?
A Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC) is part of Alberta's commercial carrier safety system. Whether you need one depends on your operation. If an SFC is required, Alberta's Pre-Entry Program must be completed before you apply for the certificate. This includes the required SFC Compliance Course and Knowledge Test.
You will also need the required safety and maintenance programs in place.
What do I need once I start hauling?
Driver files, vehicle files, Hours of Service, inspections, maintenance and repairs need to be maintained as required. Your financial records start with the first transactions too. Loads, fuel, repairs, expenses, payments and payroll all become part of the company's bookkeeping and tax records.
Do I need IFTA and IRP?
If you operate only in Alberta, you may not need them. If you expand your operation outside Alberta, IFTA and IRP may apply. IFTA deals with fuel tax for qualifying trucks operating in more than one province or state. IRP deals with registration for qualifying trucks operating in more than one province or state.
They are separate programs, and whether you need them depends on your operation.
The exact setup depends on where you go, what you haul and how you operate.
Can I start my trucking company before I buy a truck?
Yes. You can set up the business side of your trucking company before you buy a truck. You can decide whether to operate as a sole proprietor or corporation, register the business and set up the CRA accounts that apply to you.
Can I get my Safety Fitness Certificate before I have a truck?
You can complete the SFC Compliance Course and prepare your safety and maintenance programs before purchasing the truck. To apply for the Safety Fitness Certificate, however, you will need the vehicle registration information required by Alberta.
Should I set up my safety and maintenance programs before buying the truck?
Yes. Your safety and maintenance programs can be prepared before you purchase the truck. Once you have the truck and begin operating, the required vehicle, inspection and maintenance records need to be maintained for that vehicle.
What can I set up before I buy the truck?
You can establish the business, applicable CRA accounts, business structure and much of the carrier's compliance system before purchasing the truck. Requirements that depend on the specific vehicle, its registered weight or where it will operate can be completed once those details are known.
What happens after I buy the truck?
Once you know which truck you will operate and where it will operate, you can complete the vehicle-specific registration, insurance and other requirements that apply. Depending on the operation, this may include IRP, IFTA and applicable permits.
You do not need to wait until you own the truck to start setting up the company, but some parts of the carrier setup cannot be completed until the vehicle and its registration information are known.
Do I need to incorporate my trucking business?
No. You do not necessarily need to incorporate to start a trucking business. An owner-operator can operate as a sole proprietor or through a corporation.
The two structures are different legally and for income tax purposes, so the choice affects how the business is set up, how its income is reported and how money is taken from the business.
What does it mean to operate as a sole proprietor?
As a sole proprietor, you and the business are legally the same. The trucking income and expenses are reported as part of your personal income tax return rather than through a separate corporate income tax return.
You are also personally responsible for the debts and obligations of the business.
What changes if I incorporate?
A corporation is a separate legal entity. The corporation earns the business income, pays its expenses and files its own corporate income tax return.
You still file your own personal income tax return. Money you receive personally from the corporation, such as salary or dividends, is reported separately as applicable.
Does incorporating protect me from liability?
A corporation generally provides limited liability to its shareholders, but incorporation does not protect you from every type of personal liability. Personal guarantees, certain director obligations and your own actions can still create personal responsibility.
Incorporation also does not replace the insurance or regulatory requirements that apply to the trucking operation.
Is incorporating always better for taxes?
Not necessarily. A corporation and a sole proprietorship are taxed differently, but incorporation does not automatically mean that you will pay less tax overall.
The result depends on the circumstances of the business and the owner.
Does my business structure change my trucking requirements?
Your business structure and your carrier requirements are separate questions. Whether you operate as a sole proprietor or through a corporation, you still need to meet the registration, safety, insurance, vehicle and operating requirements that apply to your trucking operation.
Can I incorporate later?
Yes. You can begin operating as a sole proprietor and later incorporate the business. However, changing from a sole proprietorship to a corporation is a change in legal status, not simply a change in the business name.
The new corporation is a separate legal entity and generally receives a new Business Number. CRA program accounts such as GST/HST or payroll may also need to be set up under the new corporation, and the existing accounts associated with the sole proprietorship may need to be closed.
So which structure should I choose?
The decision should be based on the business you are actually planning to operate. Consider legal liability, income tax, administrative costs, how you will take money from the business and your plans for the company before choosing between a sole proprietorship and a corporation.
What CRA accounts does a new trucking company need?
The CRA accounts you need depend on how your trucking business is structured and how it operates. There is not one set of CRA accounts that every new trucking company automatically needs.
What is a CRA Business Number?
A Business Number (BN) is the nine-digit number used to identify your business or legal entity when dealing with CRA and certain other government programs. CRA program accounts are added to that Business Number when they are needed.
If you incorporate your company in Alberta, the corporation is generally assigned a Business Number and a corporation income tax account as part of the incorporation process. A sole proprietor does not necessarily need a Business Number until they need to register for a CRA program account.
Do I need a GST/HST account?
For most businesses, GST/HST registration becomes mandatory when taxable revenues exceed the $30,000 small-supplier threshold. A business below the threshold may also choose to register voluntarily.
Once registered, you have GST/HST collection, filing and remittance obligations. You may also be able to claim input tax credits for GST/HST paid on eligible business purchases and expenses.
Do I need a payroll account?
You may need a payroll deductions account if the company has employees or pays amounts that require payroll deductions. The payroll account is used to report and remit deductions such as income tax, CPP and EI, where applicable.
For an incorporated owner-operator, a payroll account may also be required if the corporation pays you a salary.
Does a corporation need a separate income tax account?
Yes. An incorporated trucking company has a corporation income tax program account and files its own T2 Corporation Income Tax Return.
A sole proprietor does not have a corporation income tax account for the business. The trucking business's income and expenses are instead reported as part of the owner's personal income tax return.
Does every trucking company need the same CRA accounts?
No. A sole proprietor with no employees may have a very different CRA setup from an incorporated trucking company with drivers on payroll.
The important part is to open the accounts that actually apply to your business and understand the filing and remittance obligations that begin once those accounts are active.

How do I get a Safety Fitness Certificate in Alberta?
A Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC) is part of Alberta's commercial carrier safety system. It identifies a commercial carrier that is subject to National Safety Code requirements and establishes the carrier's operating status.
Who needs a Safety Fitness Certificate in Alberta?
Whether you need an SFC depends on the type and weight of the commercial vehicles you operate and where you operate them. If you operate only within Alberta, an SFC is required for a truck, tractor, trailer or combination registered at 11,794 kg or more. If the vehicle is used or intended to transport goods outside Alberta, an SFC is required when it is registered at more than 4,500 kg.
What is the difference between Provincial and Federal Operating Status?
Provincial Operating Status applies when your regulated commercial vehicles operate only within Alberta. Federal Operating Status applies when your regulated commercial vehicles operate outside Alberta. A carrier with Federal Operating Status may operate both within Alberta and outside the province.
Your operating status matters because it determines where the vehicles operating under your Safety Fitness Certificate are authorized to operate.
What do I need before I can apply for an SFC?
New carriers that require an SFC must complete Alberta's Pre-Entry Program. This includes completing the required SFC Compliance Course and Knowledge Test. The carrier must also register a qualifying commercial vehicle, have the required safety and maintenance programs in place and complete the SFC application process.
Does the Safety Fitness Certificate belong to the truck?
No. The SFC is issued to the carrier, not individually to each truck. The carrier is responsible for the regulated commercial vehicles and drivers operating under its certificate and for maintaining the required safety and maintenance systems and records. A copy of the SFC must be carried in each vehicle that requires it.
What happens after I receive my Safety Fitness Certificate?
Receiving the SFC is the beginning of your responsibilities as a regulated carrier. Once you start operating, you need to maintain the required driver, Hours of Service, inspection, vehicle, maintenance and other safety records that apply to your operation.
New carriers that go through Alberta's Pre-Entry Program must also complete a New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) within 9 to 12 months of the SFC being issued.
Can I drive outside Alberta with Provincial Operating Status?
No. Provincial Operating Status authorizes your regulated commercial vehicles to operate within Alberta. If your regulated commercial vehicles operate outside Alberta, you need Federal Operating Status.
Is a Safety Fitness Certificate the same as commercial vehicle registration?
No. Your Safety Fitness Certificate and vehicle registration are different requirements. Having an SFC does not by itself register your truck or replace other registrations, permits, insurance or operating requirements that apply to your operation.

Do I need to complete the Alberta Pre-Entry Program before I can get my Safety Fitness Certificate?
Yes. If you are a new carrier that is required to hold an Alberta Safety Fitness Certificate (SFC), you must complete Alberta's Pre-Entry requirements before your SFC can be issued.
The process is intended to make sure new carriers understand the safety, maintenance and record-keeping responsibilities that come with operating regulated commercial vehicles.
What is the Alberta Pre-Entry Program?
The Pre-Entry Program is the process new carriers go through before obtaining their first Safety Fitness Certificate. It introduces the carrier to Alberta's National Safety Code requirements and the responsibilities that begin once the company starts operating.
Completing the Pre-Entry requirements does not replace the Safety Fitness Certificate. It is part of the process required to obtain one.
What do I have to complete before I can get my SFC?
You must complete the SFC Compliance Course and Knowledge Test, register a qualifying commercial vehicle and complete the SFC application process. You must also have the required safety and maintenance programs in place.
These programs are not just documents needed for the application. They become part of the systems the carrier is expected to follow once its vehicles begin operating.
Who has to take the SFC Compliance Course and Knowledge Test?
The course and test are completed by the registered owner or an appropriate person designated by the carrier. For a small trucking company or owner-operator, this may be the person who owns or manages the business and will be responsible for its safety and compliance requirements.
Can I start operating before I receive my Safety Fitness Certificate?
No. If your operation requires a Safety Fitness Certificate, you cannot begin operating the regulated commercial vehicles until the SFC has been issued.
Completing the course and test alone does not authorize the carrier to begin operating.
Do I need safety and maintenance programs before I get my SFC?
Yes. Carriers operating under Alberta's National Safety Code requirements must have safety and maintenance programs that apply to their operation.
These programs cover how the company manages areas such as driver requirements, Hours of Service, vehicle inspections, maintenance, record keeping and other safety responsibilities.
What happens after I receive my Safety Fitness Certificate?
Once the SFC is issued and you begin operating, you are responsible for following the safety and maintenance systems established for your operation and maintaining the required records.
New carriers that go through Alberta's Pre-Entry Program must also complete a New Carrier Compliance Review (NCCR) within 9 to 12 months of the SFC being issued. The review looks at whether the carrier is maintaining the systems and records required of its operation.
Do I have to complete the Pre-Entry Program every time I renew my SFC?
No. The Pre-Entry Program applies to new carriers entering Alberta's National Safety Code program. It is not repeated each time the Safety Fitness Certificate is renewed.
Once you are operating, the focus shifts from meeting the initial entry requirements to maintaining the safety, maintenance and record-keeping systems required for your operation.
